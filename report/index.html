Is there still a diversification benefit to emerging markets?

Team results on MSCI data from January 2001 to August 2026. Question 1: has EM become more like the U.S., and is technology the cause? Question 2: does EM do better relative to developed markets in today's crises than in past ones?

Jun Wang, Yujia Chen, Xu Han, Yaqi Xiao, Luqi Yang · monthly data 2001-01 to 2026-08, daily data to 31 Aug 2026
AnswersDataModule AModule B Module COptimizerCrisis selection Event studyPlacebo testCrisis regression

Question 1 · co-movement

EM's link to the U.S. changed once, in a post-GFC transition (point estimate Oct 2009). After it, non-tech EM's sensitivity to the U.S. fell (β 1.27 → 0.80, Holm p = 0.008), while EM's link to U.S. tech shows no significant change. In 2021–26, Taiwan and Korea carry much of EM's link to U.S. tech.

Optimizer · Modules A3 and B

EM's gain is significant only in the early 2000s. Since then the optimizer gives EM 0%, including EM ex-IT today, and adding EM does not reliably help out of sample. The optimizer's country gains cannot be told apart from chance.

Question 2 · crises

Around the 15 named crises EM lost on average 2.11 points against World over 26 trading days, more than on non-crisis dates (p = 0.044). The loss was larger in past crises (−3.36, p = 0.026) than in current ones (−1.27, p = 0.49). In the monthly regression, crisis months today are not significantly different from past ones.

Data and design

  • MSCI USD net total-return indices for EM, USA, World and USA IT; MSCI country indices for the eight largest EM markets; EM Small Cap (price and net); a local-currency EM index; the S&P 500 total return and the Bloomberg US Aggregate for the optimizer.
  • Question 1 (Modules A, A3, A tests, B, C): month-end data 2001-01 to 2026-08. Series are built in simple returns with last month's weights (EM ex-IT, EM ex-Taiwan & Korea) and converted to log returns for correlations, betas and tests. Module B also uses weekly returns.
  • Question 2: monthly log returns in % for the crisis regression and daily log returns for the event study. Crisis months: Fin = MSCI World more than 10% below its running peak (134 months); Geo = GPR or TPU in its top decile over 2001–2026, i.e. GPR ≥ 158.0 or TPU ≥ 165.2 (53 months). Current = from January 2018.

Question 1 · Module A and Module A testsEM's composition and its link to the U.S.

  • Composition. IT is 41.6% of MSCI EM in Aug 2026, against 20.9% at the end of 2001. Taiwan (27.4%) and Korea (20.8%) are now the two largest markets, ahead of China (20.6%).
  • IT risk. In 2021–26 IT carries 35.6% of EM's risk with a 23.6% weight, the largest gap of any block.
  • Link to the U.S. EM's correlation with the U.S. market falls from 0.80 in 2001–05 to 0.62 in 2021–26, and its β from 1.12 to 0.72. In the trend regression, EM's β on the U.S. falls by 0.25 per decade (p = 0.016), and EM ex-IT's by 0.32 (p = 0.005). EM's β on U.S. tech shows no trend (p = 0.97).
  • Taiwan and Korea (Module A tests). In 2021–26, removing both lowers EM's correlation with U.S. tech by 0.25 (Holm p = 0.004), against 0.02–0.03 in every earlier block; removing China raises it by 0.10 (Holm p = 0.028). The gap in correlation between EM and EM ex-IT (0.20) and the excess of IT's risk share over its weight (0.12) are also significant after Holm.

Question 1 · Module BTime-varying correlations (DCC-GARCH)

Each series gets the GARCH-type model that passes all three residual diagnostics with the lowest BIC; the bivariate DCC has its persistence capped at 0.998. The weekly conditional correlation of EM with the U.S. averages 0.60 in 2003, 0.70 in 2020, 0.55 in 2022 and 0.58 in 2026; with U.S. tech it stays between 0.55 and 0.61 in the same years.

Question 1 · Module CStructural break and primary tests

  • EM's link to the U.S. changed, and this is well supported. The sequential, UDmax and WDmax tests all reject no break, in all 12 re-searches, with or without the GFC. BIC picks one break; LWZ, the most conservative, picks none.
  • The date is a range. Oct 2009 is the joint model's point estimate (90% CI 2008-07…2011-01), inside a post-GFC transition. α moved first and β later (exploratory).
  • Non-tech EM's sensitivity to the U.S. fell (β 1.27 → 0.80, Holm p = 0.008), for break dates from 2009-10 to 2012-12 but not at 2007-10.
  • EM's link to U.S. tech shows no significant change between regimes, and EM IT stays strongly linked (β ≈ 0.9). Whether non-tech fell more than tech, and whether Taiwan & Korea raised EM's sensitivity, is not established after Holm.
  • Correlations fell for every index, but no single change is significant. A lower β is not the same as a lower correlation.

Question 1 · Modules A3, B and CMean-variance optimizer: does EM still earn a place?

A U.S. investor holds the S&P 500 and the U.S. Aggregate. The optimizer adds MSCI EM or EM ex-IT at fixed shares of 0–100% (Module A3, rolling 5- and 10-year windows ending each August), lets eight EM countries enter freely (A3, from 2015-04), and runs the same optimizer out of sample with a 60-month lookback (Module B). Module C tests whether the gains are beyond chance.

  • EM's gain is significant only in the early 2000s. In 2001–05 the gain is 0.43 (GRS p = 0.005, Holm 0.036), and even a fixed 10% adds 0.38.
  • Since then the optimizer gives EM 0% in every 5-year window from 2007-09…2012-08 onward, including EM ex-IT today. A forced 10% costs little: −0.024 for EM and −0.046 for EM ex-IT in 2021–26.
  • Out of sample, adding broad EM does not reliably improve the stock-bond portfolio.
  • Countries. The optimizer's country gains are not distinguishable from chance in any window, and the choice is unstable across bootstrap samples (Module A tests, O2).
