Is there still a diversification benefit to emerging markets?

Results of the four-step analysis on MSCI data from January 2001 to September 2026, with the focus on Question 2: does EM do better relative to developed markets in today's war and trade crises than in past crises?

Jun Wang, Yujia Chen, Xu Han, Yaqi Xiao, Luqi Yang · Data to 25 Sep 2026 · 309 months, 1,343 weeks, 6,715 days

Question 2 · crises

EM's relative performance in today's war and trade crises is not significantly different from past crises (γ4 = 0.19, t = 0.16). What moves it is mainly the dollar. Recent shocks come with a stronger dollar and weaker EM currencies, which costs EM about as much as its larger tech weight gains.

Question 1 · co-movement

EM has not become more like the US. Weekly correlation with US equities stays around 0.6 with no trend. After the one structural break (Oct 2009), EM's beta to the US fell from 1.30 to 0.85. A higher IT weight is not linked to more US sensitivity.

Portfolio · Step 4

For a US investor, minimum-variance weights gave EM 0% from 2006 to 2016, then about 50% from 2022. Over 2006–2026, adding EM barely lowered volatility (15.2% to 15.1%), and the Sharpe ratio fell from 0.63 to 0.60.

Data and design

  • MSCI USD net total-return indices for EM, USA, World and USA IT. EM Small Cap uses the price index, so it is compared with the EM price index on the same basis. A local-currency EM index tests the currency channel.
  • EM ex-IT is backed out from EM and EM IT returns with the IT weight wIT = EM IT / EM market value at the previous month end.
  • Crisis months. Fin = MSCI World more than 10% below its running peak (134 months). Geo = GPR or TPU in its top decile over 2001–2026, i.e. GPR ≥ 157.8 or TPU ≥ 168.0 (53 months). Current = from January 2018.
  • All series are aligned by calendar date. Returns are log returns in %. Regressions use Newey–West standard errors (5 lags).

Question 1 · Steps 1 and 2Is EM more like the US, and is technology the cause?

The DCC-GARCH(1,1) model (a = 0.026, b = 0.951) tracks weekly correlations. EM's correlation with the US moves between about 0.45 and 0.75. It peaked in 2009–10 and in 2020, and was 0.57–0.58 on average in 2025–26. Correlation with US IT is lower than with the US as a whole, which does not fit the idea of EM tracking US tech. EM Small Cap moves the same way at a slightly lower level.

The Bai–Perron test finds one significant break in EM's relation to US returns: the last month of the old regime is October 2009 (supF = 17.2 against a 5% critical value of 11.47; the BIC also picks one break). In equation (1), δ is negative, not positive. EM's beta to the US was 1.30 before the break and 0.85 after it (δ = −0.45, t = −3.6). EM ex-IT and EM Small Cap show the same fall. Replacing Post with the lagged IT weight also gives a negative δ. In non-crisis months it is not significant. So the growing tech weight did not make EM more sensitive to US returns.

Question 2 · Step 3 · main topicDoes EM do better relative to DM in today's crises than in past ones?

We regress EM's monthly return relative to MSCI World on the two crisis types and their interaction with the current period:

rEM,t − rWorld,t = α + γ1Fint + γ2Geot + γ3(Fint×Currentt) + γ4(Geot×Currentt) + θPostt + φ′Xt + εt

γ4 is the core test. It measures how much better (positive) or worse EM does relative to World in a war or trade crisis month today than in one in the past, in percentage points per month. γ3 does the same for financial crises. Xt holds the monthly changes in the VIX and the dollar index (DXY). The sample has 116 past and 18 current Fin months, and 16 past and 37 current Geo months.

What γ4 says

  • No significant difference. In the main version, γ4 = 0.19 (t = 0.16). With the VIX and dollar controls it is 0.40 (t = 0.31). In a war or trade crisis month today, EM's relative return is within a fraction of a percentage point of what it was in past episodes, and the estimate is far from significant. γ3 for financial crises is just as close to zero (|t| < 0.4 in every version).
  • Crisis months themselves are not special. γ1 and γ2 are close to zero too. The one strong term is θ Post (−1.3 to −1.9, t between −2.9 and −3.9): after the 2009 break, EM's return relative to World is about 1.5 percentage points a month lower than before, in crisis and normal months alike.
  • From 2022 the sign is positive but still not significant. With Current starting in 2022, γ4 rises to 1.32 (t = 1.52) and to 1.45 with controls (t = 1.63). For EM ex-IT it is only 0.54–0.66. Most of the better showing since 2022 comes through the tech sector.

Does the event study agree?

  • War and trade crises: yes. Over 26 trading days EM lost 2.4% against World in past war crises. It lost 1.9% in current war and trade crises (2.6% in wars, 1.5% in trade conflicts). The two paths in the right panel almost coincide by day +20, matching γ4 ≈ 0.
  • Financial crises: only partly. EM lost 4.4% in past financial crises but gained 1.1% in current ones. The event study suggests EM now holds up better in the first weeks of a financial shock. The monthly regression (γ3 ≈ 0) does not confirm this. There are only two current events (COVID +3.9%, SVB −1.8%). Also, Fin in the regression covers whole drawdown periods, not just the first weeks.
  • The spread across crises is large. Individual results range from −8.4% (Russia invades Ukraine) to +3.9% (COVID). The Ukraine result includes Russia's removal from MSCI EM in March 2022, which is specific to that event.

  • The dollar is the clearest driver. In equation (2), φΔDXY = −0.43 (t = −3.9): a 1% rise in the dollar in a month lowers EM's return relative to World by 0.43 percentage points. It is the only control that is strongly significant. Past Geo months (2001–2005) came with a falling dollar (−0.66% a month). Current Geo months came with a rising one (+0.25%). Current Fin months also saw the dollar rise (+0.34% vs −0.15% in the past). Holding the dollar fixed, γ4 doubles from 0.19 to 0.40, though it stays insignificant.
  • It works through EM currencies. In local currency the dollar coefficient disappears (0.01, t = 0.09). The currency part of EM's return is −0.33 points a month in current Geo months, against +0.11 in past ones. In the event study, almost all of EM's past financial-crisis underperformance is currency: −4.9 of the −4.4 total, while the local-currency result is +0.6. In local currency, γ4 is 0.55 instead of 0.19: in local terms the gain over past crises is larger, and weaker currencies take part of it away.
  • Tech partly offsets the dollar. EM ex-IT has lower γ4 than EM in every version (−0.25 vs 0.19; 0.54 vs 1.32 from 2022). In current Geo months the tech part adds +0.69 points a month (IT weight 27% on average), against −0.10 in past ones. In the event study, tech added +1.0 points in current wars but −0.6 in trade conflicts, where tariffs hit EM's tech exporters (Section 301 −1.0, Liberation Day −1.3).

Limitations

  • Few current crises. The current period has only 18 Fin months and 37 Geo months, and the event study has 2 current financial, 3 current war and 4 trade events. Single events (COVID, Russia–Ukraine) swing the group averages. Standard errors are wide: the 95% interval for γ4 runs from about −2.1 to +2.5 points a month.
  • Crisis type overlaps with time period. All 16 past Geo months fall in 2001–2005 (9/11, the Iraq war). They are war shocks in a falling-dollar, pre-tech-boom era. 31 of the 37 current Geo months are flagged by trade-policy uncertainty (TPU), and no past month is, so there is no past trade crisis to compare with. γ4 therefore mixes three things: the type of shock (war vs trade), the dollar regime, and index composition. The Post dummy (break in 2009) and the rising IT weight also change with time.
  • The Fin flag is a drawdown state. It is on for long recovery periods (Jan 2001–Jul 2005, Jun 2008–Dec 2012), so "past financial crisis months" include years of EM recovery. That is one reason the monthly γ3 and the event study differ.
  • Low explanatory power. R² is 0.04–0.16. Crisis flags explain little of EM's monthly relative return; most of the variation is outside the model.

Answer to Question 2

EM's relative performance in today's war and trade crises is about the same as in past crises. Whether it does better or worse depends mainly on the dollar: today's shocks have come with a stronger dollar and weaker EM currencies, and the larger tech weight only partly offsets this.

Step 4 · Questions 1 and 2Portfolio evaluation

A US investor holds MSCI USA and adds one EM index. Minimum-variance weights are re-estimated each month from the previous 60 months, long-only, and evaluated out of sample from January 2006 to September 2026 (249 months). Because EM was much more volatile than the US and highly correlated with it, the minimum-variance weight on every EM index was 0% from 2006 to 2016. These portfolios are therefore identical to USA only through the 2008 crisis, which is why they share the same maximum drawdown. As EM's beta and volatility fell, the weight rose to about 50% from 2022.

Robustness

  • EM Small Cap net returns, post-2010. Results with the net index are almost identical to the price index: the mean DCC correlation with the US is 0.602 in both. Equation (1) post-2010 (wIT version) is also nearly unchanged. The Post version cannot be estimated after 2010 because Post = 1 throughout. In portfolios from June 2015, USA + EM Small Cap has a Sharpe ratio of 0.69 (net) vs 0.67 (price), and USA only has 0.76.
  • Local-currency EM. See Table 1, columns 5–6: γ3 and γ4 stay insignificant, and the dollar effect disappears.
  • Current period from 2022. See Table 1, Panel B: γ4 becomes positive (1.32, t = 1.52) but is not significant at 10%.
